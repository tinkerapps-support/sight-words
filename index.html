For grown-ups

Rainbow Words is made to be played together. Your child reads, you listen and tap how it went, and the app takes care of which words come up and how much help to give.

Playing a game

Sit together and tap Play. A word shows up, your child reads it out loud, and you tap one of the three buttons at the bottom:

  • ⚡ Fast: read right away, in about two seconds, with no help.
  • ✓ Got it: right, but it took some working out or a hint.
  • ↺ Not yet: wrong, or no answer. That's completely fine.

Be honest with Fast. It's the button that tells the app a word is really learned, so when you're not sure, tap Got it. A game takes about five minutes. Tapped the wrong button? Undo fixes it.

Hints

If your child pauses, help appears on its own: the word's first letter sparkles, and a picture sentence is read aloud with a gap where the word goes, so they can fill it in. Help comes sooner for new words and fades as a word gets easier.

You can give help any time with Hint or Say it. After a Say it, tap Got it or Not yet, never Fast. The app counts a Fast with a hint showing as Got it anyway.

When a word is missed

There are no red crosses or sad sounds. After Not yet, the app says the word, spells it letter by letter, and reads it in its sentence. Let your child say it back, then tap Next. The word comes back a few cards later, and the next card is usually an easy one for a quick win. A word missed three times rests until tomorrow.

Eggs and friends

Every word has an egg in the meadow. It hatches into a little friend once the word is read Fast on two different days (after its Bubble Pops), so it never hatches on the first day. After each correct card, the egg pops up with buttons that fill as the word gets closer. A moon on the last button means "come back tomorrow". Settings → Progress shows exactly what each word needs next.

Three kinds of cards

The game changes with each word as your child learns it:

  • Bubble Pop for brand-new words. The voice says the word and your child pops the bubble with it. Hearing a word and finding it is the first step; it checks itself, so you just watch. Two first-try pops in a row move the word on.
  • Reading once a word is familiar. Your child reads it and you tap Fast, Got it or Not yet. Two Fast reads, then a Fast read on another day, hatch the egg.
  • Build It once the egg has hatched. The voice says the word and your child drags the letters into the boxes to spell it. Spelling is the step beyond reading, and getting it right on the first try is part of earning the crown.

Hear it repeats the word on Bubble Pop and Build It cards.

A few tips

  • One short game a day works better than a long one now and then. Coming back on another day is what makes words stick.
  • Stop while it's still fun. The ✕ button ends a game early and keeps everything learned so far.
  • Praise the trying, not only the right answers.
  • Every reading game earns a sticker, whatever happens.

Settings

Press and hold the ⚙ gear for three seconds (so little fingers don't open it by accident). There you can mark words your child already knows (Head start), change the game length, turn music and sounds on or off, see Progress, and save a backup. Everything is saved only on this phone, so a backup now and then is a good idea.

How eggs hatch

An egg hatches when the word is read Fast on two different days. A Fast only counts when no hint was showing (Fast with a hint counts as Got it).

  1. Meets the word (new-word card)
  2. Bubble Pop: pops its bubble on the first try, twice in a row → Getting it (spotted egg)
  3. Reading: 2 Fast reads in a row → Almost (cracked egg)
  4. A Fast read on a later day → Known: it hatches
  5. Build It: spelled right on the first try, plus a Fast read 3+ days after hatching → Mastered: a crown

Head-start words begin at Almost, so one Fast read on a later day hatches them. Not yet moves a word back at most two levels (back to Bubble Pop if it slips that far). A creature never goes back into its egg.
